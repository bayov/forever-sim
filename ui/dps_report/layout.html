    Talents
    How the talents were picked

    How. The trees are Forever's, read from the beta client's own talent tables. A talent search moves one point at a time to wherever the sim gains most against the report's level 63 boss. It keeps a move only when the gain beats the sim's noise. When two builds tie on damage, it keeps the one that ends the fight with more mana. The rotation is then tuned on the build it finds.

    A few talents are taken at full rank whatever the sim says, for movement speed or threat: Pursuit of Justice, Feral Swiftness, Subtlety (Balance), Silent Resolve (Shadow), Spirit Weapons, Suppression and the mage's school threat talents.

    Why it holds up. The search runs on the same sim, gear and fight as the ranking, so the build is the best one the sim finds for this preset, not a guess. Each new client build is checked for talent changes.

    Gaps.

    • Moving one point at a time can miss a build that only pays off once several points move together.
    • Talents the sim gives no value (threat, survival, utility) count as nothing unless they're on the must-take list above.
    • The beta stopped at level 30, so some of Forever's talent numbers at 60 aren't confirmed yet. The biggest open ones are under Assumptions on the Ranking page.
    • On the generic boss, talents that only work on some creature types (Murder, for example) stay out. The presets labelled with a creature type take them.
    Best races
    How races are compared

    How. The preset is simmed on every race its class can be, — fights each at 180s, with the same gear, talents, rotation, consumes and random seed. The racials are Forever's, as the beta client has them. The preset itself uses the best race.

    Why it holds up. Only the race changes from bar to bar, and every race sees the same random fights, so the differences come from the race and not from luck.

    Gaps.

    • Talents and rotation stay the preset's own, tuned for its race. A race that would play a little differently may come out slightly low.
    • Racials only count for damage on a boss that stands still. Utility racials (Stoneform, Will of the Forsaken, Escape Artist, threat drops) count as nothing.
    • Races within a DPS or two of each other are effectively tied.

    Strongest racials
    How racials are measured

    How. The preset is simmed with every racial off, so a race gives only its base stats, and then with one racial off at a time. A racial's bar is what turning it back on is worth. When two racials overlap, they split the difference, so the parts add up to the race's total.

    Gaps. The same as Best races: damage only, on a boss that stands still. Racials that only work on some creature types count only with Racial enemy types on.

    How the fights are simmed

    How. — fights of —s (± —s) against a level — boss with — armor and no creature type, with a full raid's buffs and debuffs. Every preset uses the same seed. The spread comes from hits, crits, procs and how long the fight runs. Each player also takes two 1,500 damage hits a minute from the boss, which push back casts and give Rage.

    Why it holds up. At this many fights the mean is good to about half a DPS, so the ranking's order isn't luck.

    Gaps. The boss stands still for the whole fight: no movement, adds, target switching, phases or deaths. Real fights land lower and vary more.

    Rotation
    How the rotation was built

    How. Each class has a rotation template. The order of its abilities is fixed, and its thresholds are knobs: the Rage a Heroic Strike waits for, when a cooldown is used, when Execute takes over. An optimizer sims each knob's values in turn and keeps a change only when it gains more than twice the sim's standard error. It repeats until nothing helps. A rotation auditor then tries hand-written alternatives in paired sims and reads the debug logs for wasted mana, Rage or Energy.

    Before the pull, buffs, totems, potions and pre-casts go up so they land at 0s, when damage starts to count. Nobody opens from Stealth or Prowl. Raid buffs and debuffs that cost a global cooldown are left to the Duty presets, except Sunder Armor, which every warrior casts once after the pull, and Hunter's Mark, which every hunter keeps up itself.

    Why it holds up. The rotation is tuned on the exact preset and fight it's ranked on, and every change has to beat the noise.

    Gaps.

    • The sim plays perfectly: no reaction time, movement or mistakes.
    • The optimizer only tunes the template's knobs. A different priority is found only if someone writes it in.
    • Where Forever changed an ability without saying by how much, the sim follows the client's data and beta measurements up to level 30. The open ones are under Assumptions on the Ranking page.

      Gear

      Synthetic Phase 1 gear.

      How this gear was made

      How. Forever hasn't revealed its level 60 raid loot, so every piece here is made up and priced like a real one. Its stats add up to Forever's budget for its slot and item level. The item levels are those of Era's Phase 1 best in slot: 66 for head, shoulders, chest, hands, legs and feet, 71 for neck, back, wrist, waist, rings, one-handers and ranged, and 76 for two-handers. The mix follows the spec's real level 60 best-in-slot sets from Season of Discovery, with Stamina at that spec's share, about 10% Spirit for mana users, and hit up to the cap. Weapons keep the damage per second of the Era Phase 1 weapon they stand in for, at the type and speed the spec sims best with. Two pieces carry the class's Forever tier set, so its 2-piece bonus is active. The two trinkets are synthetic too.

      Why it holds up. The budget is fitted on Era's level 60 epics. Forever kept Era's budget on the low-level gear its beta tuned, to within about 3%. The level 60 epics Forever has already revealed land on the same budget line as these pieces, to within about 2%. That's over 300 crafted, reputation and PvP pieces at item levels 60 and 65. Caster weapons get the double budget that Forever's real caster weapons have.

      Gaps.

      • Item level. The best real Forever gear with known stats is item level 65, plus one ring at 70. Forever's Hyjal tier sets come at 61, with no stats yet, and at 65, still hidden. If Phase 1 stops at 65, these sets carry about 5 to 6% more stats than real gear would.
      • Stamina and hit. Forever's revealed level 60 epics spend about 28% of their budget on Stamina, against about 18% here. Only about 1 piece in 20 has hit, against 4 in 10 here. So these numbers are likely a little high, most of all for specs that reach their hit cap from gear.
      • Some revealed Forever pieces carry expertise rating. These sets carry none.
      • Raid drops, the tier sets' real stats, procs and on-use effects, and Forever's launch trinkets are unknown. The synthetic trinkets stand in for them.
      • Only the 2-piece set bonus counts, not the 4- or 5-piece ones.
      Character stats
      Stat weights

      In points of the main stat. Hit and crit per rating point (10 hit or 14 crit rating = 1%).

      How the weights are measured

      How. Each stat is added on top of the preset's gear and simmed at 180s on the same random fights as the preset itself, 5,000 of them, or 40,000 where that is still noisy. A weight is the gain over the preset. Hit is also measured by taking 1% away, since a preset at its cap gains nothing from more.

      Gaps. The weights hold close to this gear. A big change in gear moves them, and hit loses its value at the cap. Stats the presets don't use, like expertise, aren't measured.

      Consumes and buffs
      How consumes and buffs were chosen

      How. Every preset gets the full raid treatment, each the best for that preset: a flask, an elixir in each slot, food, a weapon oil, stone or imbue, potions, a rune, and explosives when they gain. Only what can be had at Forever's launch counts, so nothing whose only source is a raid that isn't open yet. Only one elixir's 2% crit counts. The fight is taken to be in Hyjal Summit or the Barrow Deeps, so Forever's natural flasks work. Buffs and debuffs are a full raid's, with each spec in the party it would really be in. Each entry was also simmed with it left out, and Hide inactive hides the ones that change nothing.

      Why it holds up. Every preset gets the same treatment, so the ranking compares specs, not how much each one spends.

      Gaps.

      • A full raid with every class and every consume is a ceiling. A smaller or less prepared raid does less.
      • No world buffs.
      • Pets take no player buffs or consumes in Forever, so pet specs gain less from the raid than in Classic.
