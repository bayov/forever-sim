    Talents

    How. The talent trees come from Forever's client. A search moves one point at a time to wherever the sim gains most. It keeps a move only if the gain is bigger than the sim's noise. On a tie, it keeps the build with more mana left. The rotation is then tuned for that build.

    AI agents then tried other builds by hand, including ones the search can't reach. A change stayed only if it won head to head in the sim.

    Some talents are always taken in full, for speed or threat: Pursuit of Justice, Feral Swiftness, Subtlety (Balance), Silent Resolve (Shadow), Spirit Weapons, Suppression and the mage's threat talents.

    Why trust it. The search uses the same sim, gear and fight as the ranking. Auditor agents checked the talents against Forever's client data, and every preset against the others for fairness. Each new client build is checked for talent changes.

    Gaps.

    • The search doesn't try every build. One that only pays off when several points move together can be missed.
    • Talents with no damage value (threat, survival, utility) count as nothing, unless they're on the list above.
    • The beta stopped at level 30, so some talent numbers at 60 aren't confirmed. The biggest are under Assumptions on the Ranking page.
    • Talents that only work on one creature type, like Murder, are left out on the generic boss. Presets made for that creature type take them.
    Best races

    How. The preset is simmed as every race its class can be: — fights each at 180s, with the same gear, talents, rotation, consumes and random rolls. The racials are Forever's, from its client. The preset uses the best race.

    Why trust it. Only the race changes, and every race gets the same random fights. So the gaps come from the race, not from luck.

    Gaps.

    • Talents and rotation stay tuned for the preset's own race. A race that would play a bit differently may come out a little low.
    • Only damage on a boss that stands still counts. Utility racials (Stoneform, Will of the Forsaken, Escape Artist, threat drops) count as nothing.
    • Races within 1 or 2 DPS of each other are tied.

    Strongest racials

    How. The preset is simmed with all racials off, which leaves the race's base stats. Then it's simmed with each racial off in turn. A racial's bar is what it adds back. Two racials that overlap split the shared part, so the parts add up to the race's total.

    Gaps. The same as Best races: damage only, on a boss that stands still. Racials for one boss type count only with "Boss type racials" on.

    Rotation

    How. Each class has a rotation template. The order of its abilities is fixed, but its thresholds can be tuned, like how much Rage Heroic Strike waits for, or when Execute takes over. An optimizer tries each threshold's values in turn. It keeps a change only if it clearly beats the sim's noise, and repeats until nothing helps.

    AI agents also tried new priorities, openers and cooldown timings by hand. Each stayed only if it won head to head in the sim. An auditor agent then went over every rotation, trying other versions and reading the fight logs for wasted mana, Rage or Energy.

    Buffs, totems, potions and pre-casts go up before the pull, so they land at 0s, when damage starts to count. Nobody opens from Stealth or Prowl. Raid buffs and debuffs that cost a global cooldown are left to the Duty presets. Two exceptions: every warrior casts Sunder Armor once after the pull, and every hunter keeps up Hunter's Mark.

    Why trust it. The rotation is tuned on the same preset and fight it's ranked on. Every change has to beat the noise. Audits keep checking it as the sim and Forever change.

    Gaps.

    • The sim plays perfectly, with no reaction time, movement or mistakes.
    • A better priority may exist that neither the optimizer nor the agents tried.
    • Where Forever changed an ability without saying by how much, the sim uses the client data and beta tests up to level 30. The open ones are under Assumptions on the Ranking page.

    The sim uses the first line it can, from the top.

      Gear

      Made-up gear at Phase 1 item levels.

      How. Forever hasn't shown its level 60 raid loot yet, so every piece here is made up. Its stats fit Forever's budget for its slot and item level. The item levels match Era's Phase 1 best in slot: 66 for head, shoulders, chest, hands, legs and feet, 71 for neck, back, wrists, waist, rings, one-handers and ranged, and 76 for two-handers.

      The stats follow each spec's level 60 best in slot from Season of Discovery, with about 10% Spirit for mana users and hit up to the cap. Weapons keep the DPS of the Era Phase 1 weapon they replace, at the type and speed the spec does best with. Two pieces are from the class's Forever tier set, for its 2-piece bonus. Both trinkets are made up too.

      Why trust it. The budget is fitted to Era's level 60 epics. Forever's beta gear matches Era's budget within about 3%. Over 300 level 60 epics Forever has shown (crafted, reputation and PvP, item level 60 and 65) match these pieces within about 2%. Caster weapons get the double budget Forever's real caster weapons have. Auditor agents checked every piece against the budget, and the sets against each other.

      Gaps.

      • The best real Forever gear with known stats is item level 65, plus one ring at 70. If Phase 1 tops out at 65, these sets have about 5% to 6% more stats than real gear.
      • Forever's real epics put about 28% of their budget into Stamina, against 18% here. Only 1 piece in 20 has hit, against 4 in 10 here. So these numbers are likely a bit high, most of all for specs that reach the hit cap from gear.
      • Some real Forever pieces have expertise. These sets have none.
      • Raid drops, the tier sets' real stats, procs, on-use effects and Forever's launch trinkets are unknown. The made-up trinkets stand in for them.
      • Only the 2-piece set bonus counts.

      Stat weights

      What 1 point is worth, in the main stat. Hit and crit are per rating point (1% is 10 hit or 14 crit rating).

      How. Each stat is added to the preset's gear and simmed at 180s on the same random fights, 5,000 of them (40,000 where that's still noisy). The weight is the DPS gained. Hit is also measured by taking 1% away, since a preset at the hit cap gains nothing from more.

      Gaps. The weights only hold for gear close to this. Hit is worth nothing past the cap. Stats the presets don't use, like expertise, aren't measured.

      Consumes and buffs

      How. Every preset gets the best of everything for it: a flask, an elixir in each slot, food, a weapon oil, stone or imbue, potions, a rune, and explosives when they help. Only what you can get at Forever's launch counts. Only one elixir's 2% crit counts. The fight is taken to be in Hyjal or the Barrow Deeps, the only places where some of Forever's flasks give their full bonus.

      Buffs and debuffs are a full raid's, with each spec in the party it would really be in. Each one was also simmed without it, and "Hide inactive" hides the ones that change nothing.

      Why trust it. Every preset gets the same treatment, so the ranking compares specs, not spending. An auditor agent checked every consume, buff, debuff and enchant against Forever's client and what's out at launch.

      Gaps.

      • A full raid with every class and consume is a best case. A smaller or less prepared raid does less.
      • No world buffs.
      • In Forever, pets get no player buffs or consumes, so pet specs gain less from the raid than in Classic.
