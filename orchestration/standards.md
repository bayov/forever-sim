# Cross-class standards (orchestrator rulings)

The rules every class follows so the specs' Level 60 numbers compare fairly. Newest rulings win; this file is the reference. Each class PR states how it applies them.

## Gear

1. SUPERSEDED by 1b (2026-10-13). Was: V(ilvl) = 1.2 * ilvl - 36.4 on a 1.5-norm, spell power 0.55, slot mods 1.0 / 0.77 / 0.5625, one-hand 0.42, ranged 0.32.
1a. SUPERSEDED by 1b (2026-10-13). Was: every piece's weighted linear total = 1.73 x its item 1 budget, whatever the spec.
1b. Forever budget, per archetype (user ruling 2026-10-13: "as accurately as we can predict what phase 1 BiS sets will look like on WoW Forever on release"; adopt the special rules real epics follow). Evidence: orchestration/forever_item_budget.md (ilvl <= 30 section: Forever kept Era's low-level budget; spell power 0.86) and orchestration/bis_budget_rules.md (Era level 60 epics price every archetype on one p-norm line, p about 1.7; archetypes differ only in stats per piece; Forever about doubles caster weapons).
   - Weights: Str / Agi / Sta / Int / Spi 1; spell power 0.86 (generic), school-only power 0.70, healing on top of spell power 0.45, AP 0.5, RAP 0.4 (AP and RAP on one item count once), hit rating 1.0 (10 = 1%), crit rating 1.0 (14 = 1%), MP5 2.4. Armor free.
   - Line: L(ilvl) = 1.125 x ilvl + 2.0 (Era epics at ilvl 55-80; the user picked this fit). Slot mods: 1 head / chest / legs (T1); 0.75 shoulder / hands / waist / feet (T2); 0.5625 neck / back / wrist / rings (T3). Item levels as in item 2.
   - Armor: each piece's weighted linear total = slot mod x L(ilvl) x f, within +/-1%:

     | f | T1 | T2 | T3 |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | pure caster (mage, warlock, Shadow / Smite, Elemental, Balance) | 1.053 | 1.026 | 0.995 |
     | physical melee (warrior, rogue) and feral | 0.950 | 0.959 | 0.911 |
     | hunter (BM, MM, Survival) | 1.028 | 1.056 | 0.977 |
     | hybrid (Ret, Enhancement) | 1.093 | 1.103 | 1.048 |

     Hybrid = physical x 1.15: Forever's own ilvl <= 30 hybrid pieces carry 1.13-1.15x a physical piece's linear stats, and SoD's hybrid epics 1.15x (both about 3-4.5 stats). Era's 5-6-stat hybrids (x1.26) are not used.
     Worked totals: caster 80.3 / 58.7 / 63.0 waist / 45.8; physical and feral 72.4 / 54.9 / 58.9 / 42.0; hunter 78.4 / 60.4 / 64.8 / 45.0; hybrid 83.3 / 63.1 / 67.8 / 48.3 (T1 / T2 at 66 / waist at 71 / T3 at 71).
   - Shape check (stops the p-norm being gamed): each armor piece's 1.7-norm value, (sum (weight x amount)^1.7)^(1/1.7), must be within +/-6% of slot mod x (0.661 x ilvl + 2.92). In practice that is 4 / 4 / 3-4 stat lines per piece (T1 / T2 / T3) for casters and hunters, 3 for physical and feral, 4-5 for hybrids. bis_budget_rules.md Q5 has the worked examples.
   - Weapons, weighted linear total as a multiple of L(ilvl):
     - physical two-hander (ilvl 76): 0.90 (78.8);
     - physical one-hander (71): 0.39 each (31.9);
     - hunter melee two-hander: 0.98 (85.8);
     - hunter one-hander (71): 0.42 each (34.4; physical one-hander x the hunter / physical two-hander ratio 0.98 / 0.90, ruled 09:40 for Survival's dual wield);
     - Ret / Enhancement two-hander: 1.035 (90.6; physical x 1.15);
     - Ret / Enhancement one-hander (71): 0.45 each (36.7; physical one-hander x 1.15);
     - feral two-hander: 0.90;
     - caster staff (76): 2.21 (193.4), or caster main hand (71) 1.82 (149.1) plus held off-hand (71) 0.53 (43.4). This is Forever's caster weapon rate;
     - physical ranged (bow, gun, thrown, 71): 0.24 (19.7);
     - wand (71): 0.26 (21.3);
     - relics, idols, totems and librams stay stat-less (their effect is their value).
     Weapon damage is free and keeps the damage per second of the Era Phase 1 BiS weapon. The weapon type and speed are the ones the spec sims best with (22).
   - Trinkets: two synthetic trinkets per spec at ilvl 71 (8a).
     1. A stat trinket: 1-3 of the spec's non-Stamina stats, weighted linear total 0.56 x L(71) = 45.9 (+/-1%), 1.7-norm value 36.8 +/-6%.
     2. An on-use trinket with no passive stats, modelled with core.NewSimpleStatOffensiveTrinketEffect (sim/core/item_effects.go) on the shared offensive-trinket cooldown. Its average weighted value over a 180 s fight (uses at 0 s and every cooldown; amount x weight x seconds active / 180) is 27 for spell power and 37 for AP / Str / Agi / RAP, the sizes of Talisman of Ephemeral Power and Earthstrike. Suggested shape, 20 s every 2 min (2 uses in 180 s): about 140 spell power, or about 335 AP, or about 415 RAP for hunters, or about 165 Strength / Agility.
     Hybrids may take either kind of on-use (same price), whichever sims best. Trinkets are excluded from the 5a shares.
   - Each set reports its armor total, weapon total and the two trinkets in the PR and the presets comment, as today.

3. SUPERSEDED by 1b for budgets and by 22 for weapon type and speed. Still in force: weapon damage is free, and every spec fills its ranged / wand / relic slot with something that does something for it.
4. Hit (fairness P2): every set optimises hit against its other stats at budget prices: usually that means hit up to the spec's cap against a level 63 boss (yellow cap for melee and ranged, 16% spell miss for casters), counting talents and the 2-piece bonus, never over the cap, but if the same budget points do more as another stat (e.g. spell power for Balance), use the mix that sims best and say so.
   - Physical hit cap (2026-10-23): Forever has no hit suppression, so the yellow cap against a level 63 boss at 300 skill is the 8% base miss and the dual-wield white cap 27% (was 9% and 28%, Classic's first 1% of hit doing nothing). Evidence: wowsims/forever 3af105fe16, 4,259 white swings at 1% hit against +3 mobs missed 7.04% (Wilson 95% CI 6.31% to 7.85%), where suppression gives 8%. The physical presets built to 9% sit 1% over until regeared.
5. Spirit about 10% of the set for mana users, none for rage and energy users.
5b. Hybrids carry hybrid stats (user ruling 2026-10-13, citing Forever's developers): Ret paladin and Enhancement shaman sets mix physical and spell stats; they keep their spell power and Intellect amounts (the 5b targets in gear_targets.md, re-priced at spell power 0.86) rather than SoD's almost pure melee mix. 5a. Fair stat mix (user ruling, 2026-10-13 06:30 UTC; metric and numbers ruled 06:40). A set is not an all-in on the spec's single highest-EP stat. It carries a realistic mix: the spec's very strong stats as its core, plus lesser but still useful stats in the proportions real level 60 itemization gives the spec (the user's pointer: Season of Discovery level 60 BiS gear for the spec; the fairness auditor derives a per-spec target mix from it, report section 7b). Shares are counted linearly: a stat's share = its budget weight x amount / the set's total weighted stats (enchants excluded, AP and RAP counted once). Stamina: each spec's own SoD BiS average share at the 1b weights (gear_targets.md), +/- 1.5 points (user ruling 2026-10-13; was 18% for everyone). Spirit for mana users stays under item 5. Guardrail: no one stat over 55% of a set's non-Stamina share (raised from 45% with spell power at 0.86: real SoD caster BiS sets carry 45-54% spell power by that measure). School-only spell power only where real gear for the spec carries it. Stat weights for every preset: orchestration/stat_weights.md.

21. Rage from dealing damage is normalised in Forever, and the sim models it (user ruling 2026-10-13: "Model it + assumption"). Blizzard's Priest & Warrior deep dive: "Rage gained is now based on the speed of the weapon that dealt the damage", so AP, gear and crit damage no longer add Rage. Under the Forever ruleset, every landed white hit gives k × the weapon's swing speed in Rage:
   - k = 3.46 for a one-hander, 4.5 for a two-hander. These are beta testers' level 30 measurements (forum thread 2355684), and no level 60 number exists yet.
   - Off-hand factor 0.5 (was 1.0 "until a test shows otherwise"): a beta combat log with advanced logging (Arlewow's gist https://gist.github.com/Arlewow/a622100ad149f4fc31ddf95f9b3aab2c, linked from magey/forever-warrior issue 3, 20 September, level 13 warrior with a 1.8 sec main hand and 1.6 sec off hand dagger) shows 6.2 to 6.3 Rage a main hand swing (3.46 × 1.8) and 2.7 to 2.8 an off hand swing (3.46 × 1.6 × 0.5), glancing blows included, on about 50 and 60 clean swings (assumptions audit, 2026-10-16). Dual Wield Specialization's 10% off-hand Rage a point (client 70425 curve 111755) multiplies on top, so 0.75 at 5/5.
   - The crit bonus (+100%, Blizzard's 1 October notes; settled 2026-10-16, no longer a report assumption) adds 100% of the swing's one-hand-rate Rage, before the two-hand factor (superseded 2026-10-23, user-approved: it was ×2 on the whole swing). A two-hander crit gives (4.5 + 3.46) × speed, not 9 ×; a one-hander's crit is still ×2. Evidence: wowsims/forever 5ac4ffbcb6 and 913fb51b9f, the game files' Rule of Rage (DND) row 1322574 stating 100, with warrior and bear combat logs showing the bonus.
   - Misses, dodges and parries give no Rage (superseded 2026-10-23, user-approved: they refunded a hit's Rage as in Classic). Evidence: wowsims/forever f9f9f21883, six level 8 and 9 combat logs ("Misses, dodges and parries generate nothing").
   - Rage from damage taken is reworked in Forever too (user rulings 2026-10-16: "Check how it's supposed to scale by damage taken and use that", then "being more defensive won't penalize rage generation"). Since 2026-10-23 (user-approved, superseding 20 × (1 − a(our level)) × D / a normal mob's health at our level, with K = 20 from pre-fix level 8 to 9 logs) a hit or periodic tick taken under the Forever ruleset gives 2.5 × (1 − A) × D / c Rage (`core.ForeverDamageTakenRage`): c is the Rage conversion at the attacker's level (230.6 at 60, 243.6 at 63), A the armor assumed by the attacker's level (20% to 42, 30% at 44, 36% at 46, 38% at 50, 40% at 63, interpolated), and D the attacker's hit before any of our mitigation (armor, stance, Shield Wall, talents, block, absorbs), times the crit or crushing blow multiplier. Our level, health and armor don't count. Berserker Rage doubles it (it added a flat 2 a melee hit). At 60 a 1500 hit from the level 63 boss gives 9.2 Rage in any stance (3.4 under the old rule), where Classic gave 15.4. Evidence: wowsims/forever ee2d69b16a, 73b317c7cf and de550b7df9, post-8 October logs of warriors at 18 and 20 and bears at 30 hit by attackers from level 16 to 60, geared and naked (187 melee hits from 16 to 42); f9f9f21883 for Berserker Rage (their assumption: the game files state no amount). What D counts still rests on the earlier sources (the mechanics auditor, 2026-10-16): the 8 October beta notes, post 5 ("an expected creature's health", "increased damage taken from combat multipliers, in particular from taking critical strikes or crushing blows", absorbs ignored, armor "in the 20% to 40% range, varying by level"), the Forged in Battle warrior article ("wearing Armor will no longer reduce the Rage you gain", absorbs ignored), and, before 2026-10-23, magey/forever-warrior issue 3 for K = 20. Stances, Shield Wall and blocks aren't named by either source; report assumption `warr_dt_rage_mods` holds the alternatives, and `warr_dt_rage_k` now holds the 2.5 (measured on melee hits; spells landed at about 1 to 2 times it in their logs). It matters to tanks, and to DPS warriors through the raid damage hits (24).
   - **Level scaling of k** (user ruling 2026-10-13: "assumption, either low/medium/high (flat/midpoint/scale-like-classic)"). The constants were measured at level 30 or below. At 30 they give about Classic's Rage per swing, but at 60 flat constants give about half. So k grows with level past 30: k(L) = k30 × (1 + s × (L − 30) / 30) for L > 30, and k30 at 30 or below.
     - s = 0: flat, the Low setting.
     - s = 0.5: midpoint, ×1.5 at 60, the Expected setting and the presets' default.
     - s = 1: Classic-like, ×2 at 60, the High setting. A 2.6 s one-hander's 18.0 Rage at 60 is then close to Classic's 18.6 for an average swing.
   The level scaling (`warr_rage_k`) is a dashboard assumption in spec_assumptions.md; the off-hand factor (`warr_rage_oh`) was settled at 0.5 on 2026-10-16 (measured; user ruling "Use 0.5 ... keep assumption but locked to one value, 0.5", so the report keeps it as a one-value row). Owner: the warrior worker, in sim/core/rage.go behind `Env.IsForever()`. Bear druids get the same rule, though no DPS preset uses one.

24. Raid damage hits (user rulings 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-16: "make them real spells. Just have it be a generic class/spec agnostic setting when not face tanking"). The boss is on the tank, so a DPS takes only the encounter's raid damage, which the sim doesn't otherwise model. The encounter sets it for every player the boss isn't attacking (Encounter `raid_damage_hits_per_minute` and `raid_damage_per_hit`, sim/core/raid_hits.go): random direct hits at a set average rate, each a Fire spell hit from the boss for the set damage, ignoring resistances.
   - Every Level 60 preset and report build: 2 a minute at 1500 damage (`LEVEL_60_RAID_DAMAGE` in ui/core/preset_utils.tsx, mksettings' Level 60 encounter). The boss's tank and the leveling sessions get none.
   - A hit does what any hit taken does. It takes no health (health.go only tracks a tanked player), but it fires a Lightning Shield orb or spends a Water Shield globe (each at most once every 3.5 sec), gives a warrior Rage by Forever's damage-taken rule (21: 9.2 Rage at 60 since 2026-10-23, 3.4 before) and a 30% Enrage chance (Forever's Enrage fires on any damage taken, client 70338), and pushes back a cast (up to the time already cast, cast.go). Talents that resist pushback count where a class models them (Eye of the Storm, Healing Focus); the class workers own the rest (Burning Soul, Concentration Aura and the like).
   - Before 2026-10-16 the hits were spec options: Enhancement 2 a minute, Elemental 1 (only the shield saw them), warriors 2 at 1500 (Classic damage-taken Rage, 15.4 a hit). Those fields are deprecated.
   - The rates and the damage are estimates from Season of Discovery boss timers in the Deadly Boss Mods source (Ragnaros's Wrath every 26 sec, Broodlord's Blast Wave, Kel'Thuzad's Frostbolt Volley, Chromaggus's breaths; none on Patchwerk, Loatheb and Sapphiron; about 10 a minute on Firemaw, Vaelastrasz and Huhuran below 30%), not log measurements. The dashboard's `raid_hits` assumption runs 0 and 10 a minute beside the presets' 2.

## Weapons

22. Each preset uses the weapon type and speed it sims best with, among the speeds that Era Phase 1 BiS weapons have (user rulings 2026-10-15: "make each preset use the weapon type it sims best with", replacing the Era Phase 1 BiS type of 3, then "Use 3.7 if that's typical in phase 1 bis", "same for others"). Faster or slower weapons exist in later phases (a 4.0 sec two-hand axe, 1.3 sec daggers, SoD's 1.7 sec two-handers), but a Phase 1 BiS guess shouldn't count on them. Method:
   - Each weapon keeps its stats and the damage per second of the Era Phase 1 BiS weapon it stands in for.
   - It takes the speed and damage spread of an Era Phase 1 BiS weapon of the chosen type. Main hands: Deathbringer (2.9 sec axe), Annihilator (2.4 sec axe), Vis'kag the Bloodletter (2.6 sec sword), Dal'Rend's Sacred Charge (2.8 sec sword), Ironfoe (2.4 sec mace), Ebon Hand (2.5 sec mace), Persuader (2.8 sec mace), Perdition's Blade (1.8 sec dagger), Felstriker (1.7 sec dagger) and Core Hound Tooth (1.6 sec dagger). Off hands: the same one-handers plus Dal'Rend's Tribal Guardian and Mirah's Song (1.8 sec swords), Brutality Blade (2.5 sec sword) and Bone Slicing Hatchet (1.7 sec axe). Two-handers: any real Era Phase 1 two-hander from 3.4 to 3.7 sec (Phase 1 BiS two-handers run 3.4 to 3.8, Sulfuras is 3.7). No Phase 1 BiS list has a fist weapon, so fists are out.
   - Every class-legal candidate was simmed (180 s, 10,000 iterations) with a race that has no weapon racial. Two-handers tried every candidate. Dual wielders improved one hand at a time until nothing changed. Then every race was simmed (20,000 iterations) on the winner, with the racial's own type where it fit.
   - Builds that share a gear set share weapons only when sharing costs under 1 DPS. Otherwise the build gets its own weapon items.
   - Results (2026-10-15): slow main hands and two-handers and fast off hands win, because white hits, Mortal Strike, Heroic Strike and Slam scale with damage per swing, while per-hit procs (poisons, Unbridled Wrath) and the normalised off hand strikes (Whirlwind, Mutilate) favour fast off hands. Fury holds a 2.8 sec sword and a 1.7 sec axe. Arms and two-hand Fury hold a 3.7 sec axe, and so does Enhancement. Combat Swords holds a 2.9 sec axe and a 1.7 sec axe (Forever lets rogues use one-hand axes, forever_class_changes.md). Seal Fate Swords holds the 2.9 sec axe and a 1.6 sec dagger. The dagger rogues keep Perdition's Blade and Core Hound Tooth. Mutilate holds a 1.7 sec dagger and a 1.8 sec sword (since 2026-10-23 a 1.6 sec dagger, Core Hound Tooth's speed, in the off hand: client 70338 and wowsims/forever's talents_assassination.go ask for a dagger in both hands, and of the three Phase 1 BiS dagger speeds 1.6 sims best, 685.6 at 180 sec against 684.6 at 1.7 and 684.0 at 1.8; it is the Daggers set's off hand, item 994114). Survival holds a 2.9 sec axe and a 1.6 sec dagger. Ret ties within the noise on every 3.4 sec two-hander and keeps the 3.4 sec axe. The casters and the ranged hunters don't swing their weapon, so they keep theirs.
   - Seal of Righteousness adds a flat share of spell power to every swing in the sim, so Holy Shock sims about 50 DPS higher on a 2.1 sec two-hander (Barovian Family Sword's speed) than on the 3.4 sec one. No Phase 1 BiS two-hander is that fast, so the preset doesn't use one (notes.md, Need to Verify).
   - Ranged (2026-10-15, the user's "similar" pass for bows, guns and crossbows, run on the warrior and hunter assumption defaults of that day). The same method on every Level 60 hunter preset (Beast Mastery, Marksmanship, Lone Wolf, Survival): the ranged weapon keeps its stats and Rhok'delar's 44.0 damage per second and takes the speed and damage spread of an Era Phase 1 BiS ranged weapon (item database and wowhead's Classic data). Candidates: bows Rhok'delar, Longbow of the Ancient Keepers (2.9 sec, 89 to 166) and Striker's Mark (2.5 sec, 69 to 129); guns Blastershot Launcher (2.6 sec, 73 to 136) and Core Marksman Rifle (2.5 sec, 64 to 120); crossbows Carapace Spine Crossbow (3.3 sec, 82 to 124) and Blackcrow (3.2 sec, 77 to 117), the pre-raid BiS crossbows (no raid BiS list has one). Forever has no ranged weapon racials (Dwarf Gun and Troll Bow Specialization are gone, sim/core/racials_forever.go), so the candidates were simmed on the preset races (Night Elf, Orc for Survival; 180 s, 10,000 iterations) and the type only matters through the ammo container.
     - Results at 180 s, 10,000 iterations, Beast Mastery / Marksmanship / Lone Wolf: 2.5 sec bow 952.1 / 1020.2 / 1064.2, 2.5 sec gun 952.1 / 1020.2 / 1064.3, 2.6 sec gun 952.7 / 1021.4 / 1064.3, 2.9 sec bow 948.8 / 1017.4 / 1060.6, 3.2 sec crossbow 946.8 / 1016.2 / 1058.9, 3.3 sec crossbow 945.6 / 1014.9 / 1057.6. Aimed Shot, Multi-Shot and Sniper Shot are normalised and ammo adds the same damage a second at any speed, so speed only counts through per-shot procs (Deadly Aspects' Quick Shots), which favour the faster weapons. Survival never shoots: every candidate gives it 892.41 to the cent.
     - Winner: Striker's Mark's 2.5 sec bow (76.6 to 143.2) for the shared ranged set, item 993014, so Beast Mastery, Marksmanship and Lone Wolf share it (20,000 iterations: 952.2 / 1020.3 / 1064.0, +3.5 / +2.7 / +3.3 over Rhok'delar's speed). Survival keeps Rhok'delar's 2.9 sec bow (item 993115).

## Raid duties

23. Raid actions that cost a GCD (user ruling 2026-10-15). Long buffs (blessings, Mark / Gift of the Wild, Arcane Intellect, Fortitude, Divine Spirit, Shadow Protection, auras) are cast long before the pull and cost no GCD in the sim. The shared raid (mksettings) still provides every buff and debuff as permanent; this item says which presets pay for them.
   - **Sunder Armor: every warrior preset**, its encounter variants included, spends its first GCD after the pull on one Sunder Armor. All warriors do this and the tanks keep it up after, so there is no variant without it. The raid's Sunder debuff stays on.
   - **Duty presets.** For each DPS-raising action below, the spec gets a second preset "on duty" next to its base preset, because one player in the raid covers it and the rest don't:
     - The **base preset doesn't cast it**: the raid's buff or debuff covers it, as if another player did. If a base preset casts it today, it stops.
     - The **duty preset casts it itself**. Its request switches the raid's matching buff or debuff off, so the effect comes only from its own casts. Buffs and totems go up before the pull where the game allows; debuffs go up as early after the pull as the rotation best allows, except that a duty action that doesn't pull the boss (Hunter's Mark, like the shaman totems) may go up before the pull (user ruling 2026-10-15). The duty action is not delayed for the caster's own DPS: it goes up first, as the warlock's Curse of the Elements takes the first GCD (user ruling 2026-10-15). Each is refreshed before it drops.
     - The duty preset's rotation is the base rotation plus the duty, built from the class's rotopt template (a knob), re-optimised if the duty changes the best priorities.
     - Its meta has `duty` (a short label, e.g. "Faerie Fire") and `baseBuild` (the base preset's id), like the encounter presets' `encounter` and `baseBuild`. The report has a "Duty" toggle, on by default (user ruling 2026-10-15), that shows the duty presets in the rankings next to their base presets, each with its DPS cost against the base.
   - **The duties:**
     - Warrior (Fury, Fury 2H, Arms): Battle Shout (a raid-wide buff since client 70425, a party buff before; before the pull, refreshed).
     - Druid (Balance, Balance Wrath, Feral): Faerie Fire (Feral's is Faerie Fire (Feral)). Insect Swarm is not a duty after all, though the user approved one: both Balance builds already keep it up for its own damage (dropping it costs 65 to 72 DPS), and the raid's Insect Swarm debuff is only the target's miss chance, so a duty preset would be the base preset at no cost (druid worker, 2026-10-15).
     - Warlock (every spec): one curse duty preset, casting Curse of the Elements in place of its own curse. User ruling: a warlock holds at most one curse duty, and every curse costs the same GCDs, so one preset covers it; other warlocks cover the other curses, whose raid debuffs stay on in its request. It is Elements, not Recklessness, because under Forever Curse of Recklessness shares the minor armor slot with Faerie Fire (standards 20): with the raid's Faerie Fire up it never lands, so a Recklessness duty would have to switch Faerie Fire off for the whole raid (warlock worker, PR #30).
     - Hunter: Hunter's Mark (every preset), and Scorpid Sting (in the sting slot, in place of Serpent Sting) as a second duty preset for the ranged presets only (Beast Mastery, Marksmanship, Lone Wolf). User ruling 2026-10-15: melee Survival has no Scorpid Sting duty.
     - Paladin (every Ret preset): keeping the raid's DPS-raising judgement debuff up (Judgement of the Crusader, or whatever Forever's equivalent is), where the base preset judges only for its own damage.
     - Shaman: no totem duty (user ruling 2026-10-15). Every shaman drops its own totems, like Sunder for warriors (client 70425 made Strength of Earth, Grace of Air and Windfury Totem raid-wide; they were party auras before): every Enhancement and Elemental preset puts them down before the pull and refreshes them (Strength of Earth, Windfury Totem and Mana Spring for a melee party, Mana Spring for a caster party), and the raid has none of its own, except the second shaman's Grace of Air that every melee preset gets (standard 25).
   - **Not duties** (user verdicts 2026-10-15, one per action):
     - Demoralizing Shout, Thunder Clap, Demoralizing Roar: left to the tanks.
     - Curse of Weakness: covered by the warlock's single curse duty.
     - Judgement of Wisdom and Judgement of Light: support, not DPS.
     - Expose Armor: it doesn't stack with Sunder.
     - Improved Scorch and Winter's Chill: under Forever they help only the mage's own spells.
     - Shadow Weaving: passive.
